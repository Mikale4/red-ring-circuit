# Red Ring Circuit

**Sonic & Friends.** A fan-made Sonic platformer and fighter in voxels: thirteen zones of two
Acts each, every one ending in a boss from the Sonic Advance games; a story of twenty chapters,
*The Red Star Rings*; Sonic Battle's fighting with health for one to four players or CPUs;
training; and Emerl, who learns a move from every battle he wins. Fourteen runners, each in
Sonic Battle's sprites (or sprites drawn in its style), with that game's combos and specials.

**Play:** https://mikale4.github.io/red-ring-circuit/ -- or open `index.html` from a local
web server (`python -m http.server` in this folder, then http://localhost:8000/). It needs
WebGL, and three.js r128 from cdnjs.

Red Ring Circuit began on 2026-11-15 as the arcade cabinet in the hall of
[Post Parade](https://github.com/Mikale4/post-parade), "Post Parade Adventure 2", and became
a game of its own on 2026-11-17, taking its history with it (the early commits say "Adventure 2").

Sonic the Hedgehog, his friends and rivals and Dr. Eggman belong to SEGA. This is a
non-commercial fan project, not affiliated with SEGA or Sonic Team, and will come down on
request. The credits for every sprite sheet, track and sound are below.

No backdrop has anything hanging in the air with nothing under it: what is far off stands on
the sea, the land or the floor, or hangs from a roof. The zone list shows each zone's runner beside it. Seabreeze Cape keeps its first 224 tiles
as they were; the next five took the places of Academy Hills, Riverside Run, Sapporo
Snowfield, Nakayama Grand Jump and Red Rock Quarry, and Seaside Camp went (the cape is the
seaside now). The old stages' text is kept outside the project, in the extras folder. The
last seven are new: each opens when the one before it is cleared (its boss beaten).

**Zones, Acts and bosses.** The classic games (Sonic Retro, the Sonic Wiki, Wikipedia):
Sonic 1's zones are three Acts with the boss at the end of the third; Sonic 2's and Sonic 3 &
Knuckles' two, the boss ending Act 2 (S3&K has a mini-boss end Act 1 too). Sonic Advance has
two Acts, the first ending at the signpost and the second at the boss; after eight hits
Eggman flees and drops a capsule of animals. Sonic Advance 2 has two Acts and then the boss in
an Act of its own, a chase -- the boss keeps going and you speed up to hit it -- eight hits (six
on Easy), harder after the third or fourth. Sonic Advance 3 puts three Acts, a boss Act and two
minigames round a hub, and Gemerl comes first in Route 99's boss Act (four hits). Here a zone
is Sonic Advance's two Acts. A Star Post and rings stand just before the arena; run in and it
shuts behind you, the camera holds on it (on a chase, it runs with the boss down a long
road), and the boss comes. Each is the zone's own from the Advance games, in its own sprites
(`tools/pack-bosses.py`, from the sheets on The Spriters Resource ripped by SonicDash57, Flare,
raidlandguy, SkyLights, Shade and Sonikku), doing what it does there as far as the guides tell
it:

Eight hits each, a flash and a moment untouchable after each; past half, every boss fights
harder. Then it blows apart, Eggman gets away in the Egg Mobile, and the capsule drops: jump on
it and the animals go free, and the zone is clear. Going down in the arena puts the runner back
at the Star Post before it, and the boss back as it was.

**The main menu** comes after the title: Story, Free Exhibition, Battle, Training, Options and
Exit, in the Sonic games' look (below). **Free Exhibition** is the platformer: any runner in
any zone that is open, Act 1 or Act 2, for time, rings and Red Star Rings; the zone list shows
the Acts of the one picked above it, each with its best time and grade, and Act 2 opens once
Act 1 is cleared. A clear goes on to the next Act, or the next zone's Act 1.

**Story** is *The Red Star Rings* (`adventure-story.js`): twenty chapters with the whole cast,
each a talk, a run through a stage made for its place in the plot (thirteen of them, drawn by
`tools/genstages.py`; their goal is the Red Star Ring on its plinth), or a battle. Everyone
talks and acts as their own games have them. A chapter cleared opens the next.

**Battle** is Sonic Battle's fighting with health (60, 100 or 150): one to four fighters, any
runner, each a player or a CPU (Easy, Normal or Hard): 1P vs CPU, 1P vs 2P, CPU vs CPU, 1P & 2P
vs CPU, three-way, four-way, or 2 vs 2; everyone for themselves or Red against Blue; a KO
match or Survival (lives); a time limit or none; in any runner's arena, which the setup shows
in a picture (built from that arena's own world, once, and kept). Down guards, and held
still heals. **Training** is the same fight on a dummy that stands, guards, walks, jumps or
fights back, and never ends. **Options** set the music, sounds, screen shake and the two
players' keys (any key can be rebound; a second gamepad is player 2's).

**Emerl** (Sonic Battle's Gizoid) is the fourteenth runner. He fights with what he has
copied: each battle he wins as a player's fighter teaches him one move he does not know yet,
at random, out of the 152 the eight he copies have (Tails, Knuckles, Amy, Rouge, Shadow,
Cream, Chaos and Chaos Gamma), and the results show it as a card won. **Emerl's Skills**
(from the Battle and Training setups) sets each of his nineteen slots -- his pose, run, dash,
jump, air action, the three attacks, heavy, upper, dash, air and pursuit attacks, and the six
specials -- to his own move or one he has learned, mixed however you like; he moves and hits
with each slot's numbers, frames and effects. His sheets are Sonic Battle's, re-ripped and
organised by Dinoshi 64 (some rips by Mr. C), The Spriters Resource; `tools/pack-emerl.py`
cuts them, and the cabinet puts the frames his loadout needs onto one picture as he is picked.

**The Sonic look.** Everything in the cabinet -- title, menus, HUD, results, pause, the story's
talk -- looks like the Sonic games, not the race day's screens (`game.css`, `.adv`):
bands of blue, yellow and red, chunky outlined italic numerals, slanted panels with green
corners, the zone title card with its Act, the Advance games' ring counter and clock, and
Sonic Battle's speech box with its tail to whoever is talking.

**The cast is thirteen, drawn in 2D:** Sonic, Tails, Knuckles, Amy, Rouge, Shadow, Cream,
Chaos and Chaos Gamma, each in their own sprites from SEGA's *Sonic Battle* (Game Boy
Advance, 2003), ripped by QuadFactor for The Spriters Resource (the sprites are SEGA's and
Sonic Team's); and Dr. Eggman, Metal Sonic, Mighty and Silver, who are not in Sonic Battle, in
sprite sheets drawn in its style by TheKnucklesMainG4 on DeviantArt
([Eggman](https://www.deviantart.com/theknucklesmaing4/art/Dr.-Eggman-%28Sonic-Battle%29-Sprite-Sheet-896689723), [Metal Sonic](https://www.deviantart.com/theknucklesmaing4/art/Metal-Sonic-Sprite-Sheet-911313604), [Mighty](https://www.deviantart.com/theknucklesmaing4/art/Mighty-the-Armadillo-Sprite-Sheet-935708034),
[Silver](https://www.deviantart.com/theknucklesmaing4/art/Silver-the-Hedgehog-%28Sonic-Battle%29-896294717); free to use with credit, never to be claimed as anyone else's).
`tools/pack-sonic-battle.py` (local; the full sheets are in `tools/art/sonic-battle/`)
names every animation by its sheet's own rows -- idle, run, skid, jump, fall, landing, the
dash, the 1st/2nd/3rd attacks, the heavy, upper, dash, air and aim attacks, the shots,
powers and traps and their effects -- and packs only those onto one transparent atlas per
runner, `assets/guests/sb-<who>.png`, writing the frame tables into `adventure-sprites.js`.
Sonic's mine (Sonic Cracker) tumbles as it settles, then sits armed, blinking black, blue and
white, till something sets it off.
A body frame is anchored at the feet and centred on the head, so nobody wobbles from pose to
pose; an effect is anchored at its middle. A runner stands in the voxel world as a flat card
at a thirtieth of a metre to the pixel (Sonic about 1.1m), crisp, mirrored to face the way
they run, casting a shadow of their own shape and tinted to the stage's light. A card shows
its frame by its own UVs, so all of a runner's cards share one texture. The menu's cards are
their Sonic Battle portraits, the ones each sheet draws with a white outline. Each sheet's
address carries a fingerprint of the picture (`sb-sonic.png?v=...`), so a browser holding an
older sheet fetches the new one rather than cutting new frames out of the old layout.

**The fighting is Sonic Battle's.** That game puts every attack on B (a three-hit combo;
direction + B for the heavy and upper attacks; B while dashing, in the air, or chasing a
knocked-back foe for the dash, air and aim attacks) and its specials on R (a shot, a power
or a trap, one slotted for the ground and one for the air before each match). Here they are
three buttons, with all three specials always to hand:

| Button | On the ground | In the air |
|---|---|---|
| **Z**, light | the 1st, 2nd and 3rd attacks, a combo, and mashed on, the heavy | the air attack, three to a jump in a combo (Shadow: his Homing Attack, if anything is near) |
| **X**, heavy | the heavy attack, a smash; **Up + X** the upper attack, a launcher; **Down + X** the aim attack, a dash at the nearest thing and a blow, the chase after one in the air, or the OTG on one knocked down; while dashing, the dash attack | the air attack; **Down + X** the aim attack, or the chase |
| **C**, special | the shot; **Up + C** the power; **Down + C** the trap, and again to set it off | the air shot, power and trap |

**Battles run on combos**, the way the fighting games' do (Dragon Ball FighterZ, Marvel vs.
Capcom 3), borrowing their mechanics, not their look:

- **Blows land on their own frames.** Each attack connects on the frames where its sprite's
  fist, foot, hammer or arm is out, and reaches as far as the sprite does (`blowShape` reads it
  off the sheet). A heavy drawn swinging twice or three times hits that many times (Sonic's
  kicks, Knuckles' one-two). A move drawn mostly as an effect (Cheese's) keeps the middle of
  its frames.
- **Presses wait for their moment.** A press during a blow is kept, with the direction held
  when it was pressed, and the moment the blow lands it goes straight into the next (a
  cancel), or when the blow is over if it missed. The route: the jabs 1-2-3 into the heavy
  (just mash Z); X after any jab for the heavy, Up + X the launcher, Down + X the aim attack;
  C after anything that lands, a special.
- **The launcher, a super jump, an air combo.** Every fighter's upper throws whoever it hits
  straight up, the same five metres or so. Space as it lands jumps after them, as high as they
  will go; air attacks then chain three to a jump, and while they land both fighters fall
  slowly, the fighting games' air-combo gravity. Down + X goes after one in the air, faster
  than the aim attack and in any direction: the chase, which smashes them down.
- **Bounces, knockdowns, OTG.** A smash (the heavy, the dash attack) throws them far, flying
  low; into a wall, they bounce back up for more. The chase drives them into the floor and
  they bounce up off it. Brought down after a combo of three or more, they lie a moment, and
  only the aim attack picks them up (an OTG) before they rise, untouchable for a breath. A wall
  bounce, a floor bounce and an OTG once each a combo. One helpless in a juggle falls through
  the arena's planks, so the combo stays on the floor.
- **Long combos run down.** From the fourth hit each does a tenth less (to a third); from the
  seventh they are held a little less and fall a little faster; at eighteen they break out.
- **Weight.** Hits freeze the picture longer the heavier they are; the one hit flashes white
  and shakes through the freeze. A combo's smash, or the chase, freezes it longest, slows the
  world to a third for a moment, and the camera leans in, tilting; a launch, a bounce and a
  heavy lean it in a little. The camera keeps right up with a juggle.
- **The CPUs combo too:** jabs into the launcher or the heavy, then the chase, a super jump and
  air attacks, the OTG; Hard most often.

A double tap of a direction is the runner's own dash (Sonic Dash and Tails Hop are hops,
Knuckles' cannot steer, Shadow's leaves him unseen). Down crouches (the landing frames, as
the sheets have no crouch); Down + Space is a Spin Dash for everyone but Rouge (tap Space
to rev, let go of Down; for Rouge it does nothing), or drops through a plank. Only Sonic's
sheets draw one -- Sonic Advance's rev (twelve frames of the ball squashing and flaring its
spikes) and Sonic Battle's roll (Sonic Meteor) -- so the others spin his in their own colours:
Sonic Battle's palettes line up, his four blues where they keep their own main colour, and
the packer swaps them colour for colour (Knuckles' shoes go to his green, Tails' muzzle
white). A rev kicks up Sonic Advance's dust spray behind the runner, swelling as it builds
(each tap of Space puffs it), throws sparks once it is hard, and leaves a dust puff on launch. A jump
out of a Spin Dash or a roll stays that ball and hurts what it touches; any other jump is
the runner's own jumping and falling frames. Knuckles' climb comes from *Sonic Advance*
(Game Boy Advance, 2001; ripped by AsuharaMoon, the sprites SEGA's, Sonic Team's and Dimps'),
`tools/art/sonic-battle/knuckles-advance.png`, and the rev, dust, sparks, flare and air
crescent from its Sonic sheet, `sonic-advance.png`: their palette is Sonic Battle's rounded
another way, so the packer maps them colour for colour.

**Sonic Wave hits like a sonic boom.** Sonic spins in place, a turn at first and then a blur
(half a second), the dust spray growing behind him, sparks flying and a whine rising; then
the blast goes, with Sonic Advance's white-gold flare and a see-through crescent of air at
his front, a flash, a jolt through the picture, and a shockwave -- see-through, because it is
air breaking the sound barrier -- tearing along the ground at 28 m/s for about fourteen
metres, trailing air. It throws him back a metre, braced in his braking pose. Sonic Storm is
the same from the air, angled down.

**Every special lands with weight.** While a move winds up, energy of its colour gathers in
to a rising note: to the runner's hand, or, for Tails' Energy Ball and Rouge's Beauty Shock,
to the mouth of his cannon and to her pointing hand (measured off the frame each fires on),
from the frame the cannon or hand is there, a glow swelling at it as it fills. The release
flashes that colour, jolts the picture and, for the shots, pushes the runner back; Tails'
and Rouge's blow their sparks out of the muzzle the way the shot goes, rather than up in a
spray, and the shot leaves from it. The heaviest (Deep Impact, Chaos Burst, Major Eruption,
the Big Hand, Silent Size) freeze the world for a few hundredths of a second. Shots fly
faster and bigger and leave a trail of their colour -- Tails' Energy Ball at 20 m/s,
Shadow's Chaos Spear at 30, Amy's Pink Typhoon and Rouge's Beauty Shock see-through (wind
and sound) -- and each hit flashes and catches for an instant. Knuckles' specials raise no
dust and throw no bits: his rocks, Deep Impact, Major Eruption and Mole Bomb land on their
light, the jolt and the freeze alone. Sonic Meteor and Spiral Dive come down in dust; Girl
Tornado whirls wind round Amy; every trap goes off in Knuckles' Deep Impact explosion. Each
move's colour, flash, jolt, recoil, freeze and muzzle are in `SPECIALS` in `adventure.js`.

**Every blow lands too.** When a jab, heavy, upper, dash, air or aim attack connects, a hit
spark -- an eight-pointed star in the runner's colour round a white heart -- pops where it
met, with a flash; the picture stops dead for a few hundredths of a second and jolts, the
jabs briefly and the finishers (the third jab, the heavies, the upper, the dash and aim
attacks) longest, while what was hit shows pure white. Then it is thrown, tumbling, the way
the blow sends it (an upper straight up), and bursts where it hits something or a moment
later. Knuckles hits hardest of the six, and Amy's hammer blows land like a hammer. A blow
that meets something in its wind-up lands there and then, so a heavy's step never carries
the runner into a hurt; the aim attack's dash does the same. A Spin Dash, roll or Homing
Attack that hits throws its mark the way it was going, a stomp sparks underfoot, and a
special's blast throws what it hits away from it. Each blow's freeze, jolt, spark and knock
are in `HITS` in `adventure.js`.

Everyone sinks in deep water and has sixteen seconds of breath unless they find an air
vent's bubbles, as in the classic games; only Tails swims (by flying), and Chaos, who is
water. Every jump clears two metres (Knuckles' and Chaos's only just). Run speeds follow
Sonic Battle's ratings (Shadow 8, Sonic 7, Tails and Amy 6, Rouge 5, Cream 4, Knuckles 3,
Gamma 2 walking, Chaos 1, of 9); Eggman, Metal Sonic, Mighty and Silver, who are not in it,
are set by their own games (Metal built to outrun Sonic, Mighty and Silver about Amy's,
Eggman a little quicker than Rouge). Sources: Sonic Center's Sonic Battle character FAQ and
the Green Hill Zone's Sonic Battle page for the moves; TheKnucklesMainG4's sheets for the
four not in it; Sonic Retro, StrategyWiki and Wikipedia for the platformers.

**The badniks** are Eggman's robots from *Sonic Advance*, *Sonic Advance 2* and *Sonic
Advance 3* (Game Boy Advance), in their own sprites from the Badniks sheets ripped by
SpitfyaUK and Techokami, Fatazz and SkyLights (The Spriters Resource); the sprites are
SEGA's, Sonic Team's and Dimps'. `tools/pack-badniks.py` (local; the sheets are in
`tools/art/sonic-advance-badniks/`) cuts the frames of the twenty-three the stages use onto
one atlas, `assets/guests/badniks.png`, each frame anchored where it stands. Each does what
Sonic Retro says it does in its own game: Gami Gami walks sideways and fires a ball from
each claw; Rhinotank charges what it sees ahead; Stinger, Oct and Slot fly to and fro and
stop to fire at you, straight down, or drop a coin; Kiki lobs bombs and Guards fire them
from their trumpets; Hanabii launches fireworks that burst overhead; Yukimaru and Pen throw
snowballs; Leon shoots out its tongue and Piko-piko brings down its mallet, close up; Mogu
hides in its hole and pops out; Aotento lifts off when you come near and Condor dives at you
from above. Some cannot be hit every way: Senbon's spikes, a spinning Star and a shut Mirror
hurt whatever touches them, so wait till they open; Pierrot's ball and Gao-gao's face take
any blow, so jump on them or come in curled (or from behind); a blast (a special) beats them
all. Beaten, a badnik goes up in Sonic Advance's puff of smoke and its animal goes free.

**The rules are the rings'.** Rings (in voxels) are your health: a hit throws them out of you
(thirty-two at most, to be caught again before they fade); a hit with none, a pit, or running
out of breath puts you back at the last Star Post. Item monitors -- Sonic Advance 3's, in its
sprites (`tools/pack-items.py`, ripped by SpitfyaUK and Techokami) -- hold a Super Ring (ten
rings), a Shield (it takes one hit), a Magnetic Shield (rings fly to you), Invincibility
(fifteen seconds, sparkles circling you like a sphere) or Speed Shoes. Springs are voxel,
Sonic Advance 3's red spring. Beaten badniks let their animal go. Each zone hides three Red
Star Rings across its Acts. The grade (S to C) weighs the time against the Act's par, the
share of rings, and whether you fell. Clearing a zone opens the next, and a zone once cleared
stays open; a clear from before the zones had Acts counts for both. Best times, grades, Red
Star Rings, the story's progress and Emerl's moves are kept in this browser
(`redringcircuit.save`); on a browser where the game was played as Post Parade's cabinet, that
record comes along the first time, and so do the settings and the two players' keys.

**The title screen** comes first. Behind it the stage you are on goes by on its own, from
its start to its end and round again; over it the Red Ring Circuit logo under its Sonic & Friends banner, Press Enter,
Start, and your record (zones cleared, Red Star Rings found, S grades). Along the bottom the whole cast goes by in a
post parade -- the fourteen of them in a line, in their own running frames, along the rail
on a strip of mown turf, Eggman's Egg Mobile floating over it -- with the line that says
it is a fan-made cabinet and the characters are SEGA's.

**Controls.** Arrows or WASD to run (and Up and Down to pick a move, crouch or climb), Space
to jump (hold for height), Z, X and C to attack. Enter goes on from the title to the main
menu, starts the Act from the zone select, and opens and closes the pause menu (Esc pauses
too). In the menus the arrows move between the choices -- to the nearest one that
way, with a gold outline round it -- Z confirms and X goes back: to the main menu from the zone select, back into the run from the pause menu, to the zone
select from the results. A gamepad works (stick or d-pad; A jumps,
X light, Y heavy, B special; Start pauses). On a phone there are buttons on screen -- a pad
of four, and Light, Heavy, Special and Jump -- and the camera backs off on a tall screen so
twelve metres fit across.

**Stages are text** (`adventure-stages.js`): chunks of 32 rows, one character a one-metre
tile, with the legend at the top of the file (`E` is the left edge of a boss's arena). Each
zone is written as its two Acts, cut from the one stage at its middle Star Post.
`tools/genstages.py` (local) draws them from
set pieces -- runs, hills, climbs, pits with stepping stones, high roads, vaults, balloon
ladders, bumper pits -- and writes the file; `node tools/validate.js` checks them,
and `tools/dev/adventure-bot.js` plays them through in a test page. Besides the old pieces
there are conveyor belts (`{` `}`), bumpers (`Q`) and balloons (`o`, which pop and throw you
up, your air moves back, as Sonic Advance's do). The stage is built in sixteen-tile voxel
chunks at the lobby's quarter-metre grain, with a deep top strip along the surface (grass, a
rug, snow, carpet, road with its lanes, marble squares, moss, sand, steel), each theme's own
blocks (chalk-grey stone, riveted steel with hazard stripes, books on end, carved stone with
glowing glyphs, dark glass edged in neon, concrete, white marble with a gilt band, mossy
carved stone, red sandstone in bands, burnt concrete with the fire in its cracks, hull
plating with its lights) and ledges (planks, grating, shelves, a gold rail, red girders), caves and roofed rooms backed with a wall, things that break or crumble as their
own meshes, rings as one instanced mesh, and each theme's scenery set back so the
perspective camera gives it parallax.

**Music and sounds are the Sonic games' own.** The music is in `audio/`: tracks from the soundtracks of
*Sonic the Hedgehog 4 Genesis*, *Sonic 3 Angel Island Revisited*, and the *Sonic 2* and *Sonic
1 Anniversary Editions* (khinsider), at 96 kbps. Each zone has its two Acts' themes --
Seabreeze Cape Splash Hill, Workshop Airstrip Sky Fortress, Rose Parlour Spring Yard, Guardian
Peak Ice Cap, Diamond Club Casino Night, Sundown Expressway Star Light, Petal Hall Marble,
Moonwell Springs Hydrocity, Redstone Canyon Sandopolis, Cinder City Lava Reef, Iron Hold
Metropolis, Comet Circuit the Half-Pipe special stages, Egg Orbital Death Egg -- and the story's
stages take their place's. The bosses fight to Sonic 3's boss theme (Gemerl to Sonic &
Knuckles' mini-boss, Mecha Knuckles to Sonic 4's Metal Sonic battle, the Egg Frog to Big Arms,
the two chases to Sonic 4's boss theme, its second phase past half). The title, main menu,
zone select, options and story have their own; Battle has Sonic 3's competition menu, its five
competition stages by arena, and its results; Training the Glowing Spheres. The jingles are
there too: the Act clear, invincibility (over whatever plays, which picks up again where it
left off), and the drowning count. The sound effects are Sonic 3 & Knuckles' (Mr Lange's Sonic
Sounds Complete rips, named from the Sonic Retro disassembly): jump, the rings right and left,
ring loss, spring, Spin Dash (higher as it revs), roll, dash, Star Post, signpost, shield,
bubble, splash, bumper, break, explosions, the boss's arrival, hit and thud, and more; the few
the games have no sound for are synthesised. The other games' rips are in `tools/art/sound-sfx`
(local), the Genesis ones named the same way; the Advance and Sonic Battle ones are numbered
only, waiting for names.

# Red Ring Circuit

**Sonic & Friends.** A fan-made Sonic platformer and fighter in voxels: thirteen zones of two
Acts each, every one ending in a boss from the Sonic Advance games; a story, *The Red Star
Rings*, told in an episode for each of the cast; **Red Ring Circuit**, a grand prix of six cups of
four racecourses, eight runners at a time, with Red Rings that hold a power each; Sonic Battle's
fighting with health for one to four players or CPUs; tournaments of 8 or 16; training; Emerl, who
learns his friends' moves in his own episode; a team to win over, fighter by fighter; and an
online lobby -- accounts, friends, messages, chat, and rooms for battles, races, the grand prix,
the story together and tournaments. Fourteen runners, each in Sonic Battle's sprites (or sprites
drawn in its style), with that game's combos, specials and effects.

**Play:** https://mikale4.github.io/red-ring-circuit/ -- or open `index.html` from a local
web server (`python -m http.server` in this folder, then http://localhost:8000/). It needs
WebGL, and three.js r128 from cdnjs.

Red Ring Circuit began on 2026-11-15 as the arcade cabinet in the hall of
[Post Parade](https://github.com/Mikale4/post-parade), "Post Parade Adventure 2", and became
a game of its own on 2026-11-17, taking its history with it (the early commits say "Adventure 2").

Sonic the Hedgehog, his friends and rivals and Dr. Eggman belong to SEGA. This is a
non-commercial fan project, not affiliated with SEGA or Sonic Team, and will come down on
request. The credits for every sprite sheet, track and sound are below.

No backdrop has anything hanging in the air with nothing under it: what is far off stands on
the sea, the land or the floor, or hangs from a roof. The zone list shows each zone's runner beside it. All thirteen were drawn again on
2026-11-18, the grand prix's way (each a place of its own, built from its own pieces, roads high
and low, secrets, a set piece to end each Act, and harder zone by zone). Zones two to six took the places of Academy Hills, Riverside Run, Sapporo
Snowfield, Nakayama Grand Jump and Red Rock Quarry, and Seaside Camp went (the cape is the
seaside now). The old stages' text is kept outside the project, in the extras folder. The
last seven are new: each opens when the one before it is cleared (its boss beaten).

**Zones, Acts and bosses.** The classic games (Sonic Retro, the Sonic Wiki, Wikipedia):
Sonic 1's zones are three Acts with the boss at the end of the third; Sonic 2's and Sonic 3 &
Knuckles' two, the boss ending Act 2 (S3&K has a mini-boss end Act 1 too). Sonic Advance has
two Acts, the first ending at the signpost and the second at the boss; after eight hits
Eggman flees and drops a capsule of animals. Sonic Advance 2 has two Acts and then the boss in
an Act of its own, a chase -- the boss keeps going and you speed up to hit it -- eight hits (six
on Easy), harder after the third or fourth. Sonic Advance 3 puts three Acts, a boss Act and two
minigames round a hub, and Gemerl comes first in Route 99's boss Act (four hits). Here a zone
is Sonic Advance's two Acts. A Star Post and rings stand just before the arena; run in and it
shuts behind you, the camera holds on it (on a chase, it runs with the boss down a long
road), and the boss comes. Each is the zone's own from the Advance games, in its own sprites
(`tools/pack-bosses.py`, from the sheets on The Spriters Resource ripped by SonicDash57, Flare,
raidlandguy, SkyLights, Shade and Sonikku), doing what it does there as far as the guides tell
it:

Eight hits each, a flash and a moment untouchable after each; past half, every boss fights
harder. Then it blows apart, Eggman gets away in the Egg Mobile, and the capsule drops: jump on
it and the animals go free, and the zone is clear. The capsule stands 1.75 m after every boss,
under anyone's plain jump (the lowest, Knuckles' and Chaos', tops out at about 2.1 m). Going down in the arena puts the runner back
at the Star Post before it, and the boss back as it was.

**The main menu** comes after the title: Story, Red Ring Circuit, Free Exhibition, Battle,
Tournament, Online Lobby, Training, Options and Exit, in the Sonic games' look (below), under a
bar with your player card, your friends and your messages, and the Fighters band. Behind it the
title's demo runs on (the title's background, without the four on the strip). **Free Exhibition**
is the platformer: any runner of your team in any zone that is open, Act 1 or Act 2, for time,
rings and Red Star Rings; the zone list shows the Acts of the one picked above it, each with its
best time and grade, and Act 2 opens once Act 1 is cleared. A clear goes on to the next Act, or
the next zone's Act 1. Its other half is **Stage Racing**: a zone's Act 3 against a rival.

**The team.** Every account starts with Sonic, Tails and Emerl; the other eleven are won over,
each in a way of their own and in keeping with the story: Amy and Knuckles join through Sonic's
story, Chaos through Tails', Cream through Emerl's, Gamma through Amy's; Shadow waits at Sundown
Expressway for a one-on-one once you've won ten battles (and cleared "Prove It"); Rouge comes for
the Red Star Rings once you've found fifteen; Silver waits at the end of Cinder City Act 2 (after
"The Wrong Hedgehog"); Metal Sonic wants a race through Comet Circuit Act 3; Mighty joins when
you've freed 300 animals from badniks and capsules; and Eggman holds a tournament of his own, the
Eggman Cup (a bracket of 8, him waiting in the final), once you've cleared the last chapter of
any story. Every unlock has its scene and its card; a challenge waits under **Fighters** (the
main menu's band: who's on the team, what each of the rest asks, and how far along you are) until
you take it on. Fighters not on the team show as silhouettes named "???" everywhere you'd pick
them, but can still be CPUs. Saves from before the team count the story chapters already
cleared. The battle arenas open as their zones are reached (Battle has a Random arena too), and
the 16-place tournament opens once you've won an 8.

**Act 3 and Stage Racing.** Every zone has an Act 3: its racecourse, much longer than Acts 1 and
2 (about a thousand metres), drawn the grand prix's way: the zone's own pieces at full stretch
(Seabreeze's coves and lighthouse cable, the airstrip's catapults and runway, the parlour's
curtain rail and drainpipe, and so on), few badniks, a low road with the zone's trouble on it,
springs up to a middle road and a high road over that (every spring with clear sky over it, so
nothing it throws you into stops you dead), and shortcuts: walls to go over by their steps or
cracked rock to roll through and a tunnel straight on, ramps over gaps the low road goes round. Stage Racing (Free Exhibition) races a
zone's Act 3 against a rival of your choosing at Easy, Normal or Hard; several chapters of the
story are races too, and so is Metal Sonic's challenge. The rival is a runner on the course with
you, as you are (the robot, below, at its hands): it takes the rings it runs through, lights the
Star Posts it passes, breaks monitors and gets what's in them, is hit and loses its rings, and
falls and starts again from its last Star Post. Easy holds its top speed back and it never
dashes; Hard is flat out, its own dash and all.

**Red Ring Circuit** is the grand prix, run the way Mario Kart 64 runs its own: pick a class
(Breeze, Gale or Storm: how fast the rivals go and how keen they are on powers), your runner and a
cup -- the cup select shows each cup's four courses in pictures -- and race its four courses
against seven of the cast, eight on the line. Points go 9, 6, 3 and 1 to the first four; fifth
or worse and the race is run again. After each race come its results (the order home with
everyone's time) and the standings, the points counting up and everyone moving to their new
place; after the fourth, the award ceremony: the top three on the podium, and a gold, silver or
bronze trophy if you're one of them (kept per cup and class, on the cup select). Cups open one
after another, a trophy in the one before opening the next, and Storm opens with a gold at Gale.
The six cups, twenty-four courses, each drawn by hand as a place of its own -- a journey through
it in four legs (a Star Post between them: the place, then its roads opening up and its secret,
then its own thing at full stretch, its set piece last), several roads through most of it (the
high road quick for those who get up there, the low road with its secrets), and one thing at
its heart nobody else has. Each cup is harder than the last: more roads and secret rooms (seven
in the Flicky Cup, thirty-one in the Emerald), more badniks (the cup's own, from seventeen to
forty-eight), more thorns and more falls to nothing, bigger set pieces -- each cup's danger its
own kind (ice and freezing water in the Aurora, the sky under you in the Skyward).

**The courses' machinery**, beside the ground and its springs, dash strips, bumpers, balloons,
belts, ice, mud and water: **loops** and **corkscrews** (run in at speed and round you go -- round a loop on your feet,
upside down over the top; too slow and you run on under them), **zip lines** (pulleys, vines, cables between airships: run into
the handle and hang on; the cable car goes up), **rails** to grind (coasters, mine railways, the
monorail, ice rails; downhill you gain, Jump leaves them), **pipes** (in at the mouth and through
at speed), **cannons** (in, a moment, and fired true: nothing steers a shot till it lands),
diagonal **springs**, water **currents** that carry you, **wind** at your back or in your face,
**low gravity**, **spouts and geysers** that throw you up when they go off, and **dust devils**
that wander a stretch and lift whoever they reach.

The leaders get traps and defences, the middle things to throw, the back of the pack the big
ones that bring you back into it. A hit sends a runner spinning a moment (a shield takes it
instead, and a runner with rings drops them); rolling or attacking into a rival knocks them down
too. The rivals are CPU runners on the course with you, as a race's rival is: each a little
quicker or slower than the next (Breeze: about nine tenths of your top speed and no dash; Gale:
a little under yours, dashing now and then; Storm: flat out), the pack kept near you, taking the
rings, Red Rings and monitors they run through and lighting the Star Posts, riding the courses'
machinery as you do, and using powers of their own (a runner who can fly or glide only does it
over water, a fall or a wall: flying is slower than running). The HUD shows your place, the
whole field along the course on a strip, and the power box.

**Story** is *The Red Star Rings*, told the way Sonic Battle tells its story: one plot, an
episode for each of the fourteen. Eggman means to set every Red Star Ring in his Star Engine
aboard the Egg Orbital, and each episode is those same days from one runner's side, with their
own scenes, runs and battles (seven to ten chapters each). Pick whose episode first, then its
chapters: each a talk, then a run through a stage made for its place in the plot (thirteen of
them, drawn by `tools/genzones.py`, their goal the Red Star Ring on its plinth), a **zone**, or a
battle as the episode's runner, sometimes with an ally. A zone chapter is played the way the
zones are: Act 1, a word between, Act 2, and at Act 2's end its boss -- Eggman's robot, or, more
often, one of the cast waiting in the boss's arena: the screen shuts as it does for a boss, they
drop in, the two talk face to face, VS, and the stage gives way to the fight in that zone's own
arena. Clear Act 1 and the chapter goes on from Act 2 next time; reach the fight, and from the
fight. Some chapters are races instead: the zone's Act 3 against one of the cast (Sonic and
Shadow down Sundown Expressway, Amy and Sonic through Rose Parlour for a date, Rouge and Knuckles
over Diamond Club's rooftops, Mighty and Sonic round Redstone Canyon, Silver after Metal Sonic up
Comet Circuit). Every episode has a mix of them. In the scenes each speaker's bust stands on their
side behind the talk box, the two facing each other (a runner standing in the stage turns to
whoever is talking to them), and moves as they talk: a hop when they're pleased, a laugh, a
shake when they're angry, a jump back at a shock, a tilt at a question, a slump at a sigh, a lean
in to make a point, and a jittering run off the screen when someone makes their getaway. The
small cards elsewhere crop each bust round its face. Everyone talks and acts as their own games have

**Battles run on combos**, the way the fighting games' do (Dragon Ball FighterZ, Marvel vs.
Capcom 3), borrowing their mechanics, not their look:

- **Blows land on their own frames.** Each attack connects on the frames where its sprite's
  fist, foot, hammer or arm is out, and reaches as far as the sprite does (`blowShape` reads it
  off the sheet). A heavy drawn swinging twice or three times hits that many times (Sonic's
  kicks, Knuckles' one-two). A move drawn mostly as an effect (Cheese's) keeps the middle of
  its frames.
- **Presses wait for their moment.** A press during a blow is kept, with the direction held
  when it was pressed, and the moment the blow lands it goes straight into the next (a
  cancel), or when the blow is over if it missed. The route: the jabs 1-2-3 into the heavy
  (just mash Z); X after any jab for the heavy, Up + X the launcher, Down + X the aim attack;
  C after anything that lands, a special (the one reeling waits for the shot to come out).
- **Shots in a combo.** A shot that lands opens the next move: the rest of the special (or
  Sonic's recoil) cuts short into whatever is pressed -- Down + X the chase, which goes up with
  them, Space a super jump into an air combo, a dash, a blow, the power. Jab, jab, shot, chase
  is four hits for anyone; jab, jab, shot, super jump, air, air, chase six to eight. Shot point
  blank, a shot still hits (it sweeps from the hand out to the muzzle). Knuckles' rock is
  lobbed over anyone close: his lands on them at range.
- **The launcher, a super jump, an air combo.** Every fighter's upper throws whoever it hits
  straight up, the same five metres or so. Space as it lands jumps after them, as high as they
  will go; air attacks then chain three to a jump, and while they land both fighters fall
  slowly, the fighting games' air-combo gravity. Down + X goes after one in the air, faster
  than the aim attack and in any direction: the chase, which smashes them down.
- **Bounces, knockdowns, OTG.** A smash (the heavy, the dash attack) throws them far, flying
  low; into a wall, they bounce back up for more. The chase drives them into the floor and
  they bounce up off it. Brought down after a combo of three or more, they lie a moment, and
  only the aim attack picks them up (an OTG) before they rise, untouchable for a breath. A wall
  bounce, a floor bounce and an OTG once each a combo. One helpless in a juggle falls through
  the arena's planks, so the combo stays on the floor.
- **Long combos run down.** From the fourth hit each does a tenth less (to a third); from the
  seventh they are held a little less and fall a little faster; at eighteen they break out.
- **Weight.** Hits freeze the picture longer the heavier they are; the one hit flashes white
  and shakes through the freeze. A combo's smash, or the chase, freezes it longest and slows
  the world to a third for a moment. The camera keeps right up with a juggle.
- **The dynamic camera** (one-on-one fights only) leans in, tilted -- in, held, and eased out
  -- for the moments worth it: a KO (the last, match-deciding one biggest and slowest), the
  smash or chase ending a combo of five hits or more, or a heavy one, or one on someone low on
  health; a wall or floor bounce six hits into a combo; a parry or a burst out of a long combo
  in a tight spot (a quarter of the health left). Then it rests for a couple of seconds (a KO
  still gets one), and it never leans past the arena's walls, so a fight in a corner holds
  still. With three or four fighters it keeps still, framing them all.
- **Framing, on any screen.** The camera keeps everyone inside the strip of the picture between
  the health cards and, on a touch screen, the pad's buttons, so no one stands under them. On
  an upright phone it backs off much further; if everyone still won't fit, it keeps Player 1
  in, leaning toward their nearest foe, and anyone off the picture has their tag held at the
  edge, pointing their way.
- **Guard and parry (Sonic Battle's).** Down tapped is the fighter's own guard skill, as long as
  theirs is in Sonic Battle (Rouge's quick, behind her wings; Gamma's Guard Mode long; the Chaos
  Guard strong; Cream's Chao Knight, Cheese in front of her; Metal's and Eggman's shields). A blow
  that meets it in its first eighth of a second is parried: nothing comes of it, and whoever threw
  it reels ("Attack blocked"), open to a counter that does a quarter more. Later in the guard, a
  plain block. Held still on Down, they heal, in their own healing frames. The guard shows Sonic
  Battle's barrier -- a swirl that opens into a pink ring round them and fades over its half
  second -- a blocked blow flashes on it, and healing has the green ring rising round them.
- **Burst (Guilty Gear's Psych Burst).** **Z + X** together with the burst gauge full (under the
  health). Caught in a combo, knocked down or reeling, it is a blue burst: a blast that throws
  everyone near away, does no damage, and ends the combo. Otherwise it is gold: it hits, and
  landing it gives a third of the gauge back. The gauge starts full and fills with time, with
  damage taken, and with every guard and parry -- most for guarding a special, as Sonic Battle
  fills its gauge.
- **Every hurt has its frames,** from rows of the sheets the game had not used: knocked upward off
  a launch, knocked back (tumbling) off a smash, hitting a wall, hitting the ground, getting up,
  the attack blocked. A knockdown ends in getting up, untouchable for a moment. A fighter who loses
  a life is thrown tumbling, lands flat out on the ground, flickers, and is gone till they drop
  back in. "Parry!", "Burst!"
  and "Counter!" pop up over whoever did it, and the hit counter shows in every fight.
- **The CPUs play it properly.** A CPU never throws a blow at nothing: it attacks only with its
  mark in reach, on its level and open (not untouchable, not lying down but for an OTG). It goes
  where its mark is: up after one on a plank above (a jump and its air action at the top), down
  through or off its own plank after one below; one it can't get up to it waits for under the
  plank, its launcher ready for them coming down. Each fights in a style, mostly its runner's:
  **rush** (in close and combo: Sonic, Shadow, Knuckles, Amy, Mighty, Metal, Emerl), **zone**
  (keep off at shot range and fire, back away and leave traps: Tails, Cream, Gamma, Eggman,
  Silver) or **counter** (hover just outside reach, guard and parry, punish: Rouge, Chaos);
  anyone left open (parried, reeling, whiffing up close) is punished whatever the style. Its
  combos are planned and pressed as a player presses them, the next blow only once the last has
  landed: jabs into the launcher, a super jump, an air combo and the chase; the auto combo into
  the smash; jabs into the heavy, a special or the aim attack. A blow that misses ends it. Easy
  drops combos now and then, Normal now and again, Hard never; Hard guards and parries most, and
  bursts out of long combos soonest. It sees a shot coming and guards it in time: Hard parries
  nearly every one, Normal blocks about half, Easy few.

A double tap of a direction is the runner's own dash (Sonic Dash and Tails Hop are hops,
Knuckles' cannot steer, Shadow's leaves him unseen). Down crouches (the landing frames, as
the sheets have no crouch); Down + Space is a Spin Dash for everyone but Rouge (tap Space
to rev, let go of Down; for Rouge it does nothing), or drops through a plank. Only Sonic's
sheets draw one -- Sonic Advance's rev (twelve frames of the ball squashing and flaring its
spikes) and Sonic Battle's roll (Sonic Meteor) -- so the others spin his in their own colours:
Sonic Battle's palettes line up, his four blues where they keep their own main colour, and
the packer swaps them colour for colour (Knuckles' shoes go to his green, Tails' muzzle
white). A rev kicks up Sonic Advance's dust spray behind the runner, swelling as it builds
(each tap of Space puffs it), throws sparks once it is hard, and leaves a dust puff on launch. A jump
out of a Spin Dash or a roll stays that ball and hurts what it touches; any other jump is
the runner's own jumping and falling frames. Knuckles' climb comes from *Sonic Advance*
(Game Boy Advance, 2001; ripped by AsuharaMoon, the sprites SEGA's, Sonic Team's and Dimps'),
`tools/art/sonic-battle/knuckles-advance.png`, and the rev, dust, sparks, flare and air
crescent from its Sonic sheet, `sonic-advance.png`: their palette is Sonic Battle's rounded
another way, so the packer maps them colour for colour.

**Sonic Wave hits like a sonic boom.** Sonic spins in place, a turn at first and then a blur
(half a second), the dust spray growing behind him, sparks flying and a whine rising; then
the blast goes, with Sonic Advance's white-gold flare and a see-through crescent of air at
his front, a flash, a jolt through the picture, and a shockwave -- see-through, because it is
air breaking the sound barrier -- tearing along the ground at 28 m/s for about fourteen
metres, trailing air. It throws him back a metre, braced in his braking pose. Sonic Storm is
the same from the air, angled down.

**Every special lands with weight.** While a move winds up, energy of its colour gathers in
to a rising note: to the runner's hand, or, for Tails' Energy Ball and Rouge's Beauty Shock,
to the mouth of his cannon and to her pointing hand (measured off the frame each fires on),
from the frame the cannon or hand is there, a glow swelling at it as it fills. The release
flashes that colour, jolts the picture and, for the shots, pushes the runner back; Tails'
and Rouge's blow their sparks out of the muzzle the way the shot goes, rather than up in a
spray, and the shot leaves from it. The heaviest (Deep Impact, Chaos Burst, Major Eruption,
the Big Hand, Silent Size) freeze the world for a few hundredths of a second. Shots are big
and leave a trail of their colour, at 8 to 18 m/s (the Mighty Cyclone slowest, the Chaos
Spear fastest; Tails' Energy Ball 12) -- slow enough to see coming and guard, Amy's Pink
Typhoon and Rouge's Beauty Shock see-through (wind and sound). Each flickers as the Egg
Mobile's booster does: it grows in, then a new frame of its body every thirtieth of a second
in no steady order, now and then a frame dropped, and one that runs out dies away through its
last frames. A shot stops in the fighter it hits (or a boss), with weight: the picture
freezes and jolts, a ring of light swells, and sparks blow out the way it was going. A guard
catches it. Badniks and blocks it breaks, it goes on
through. Every shot throws its mark its own way (`kn` in `SPECIALS`: how far, how high, how
long they float coming down): the Mighty Cyclone launches tallest (about six metres) and Amy's
Pink Typhoon next, both straight up as the launcher does; the Chaos Spear and Gamma's
Devastator (more, charged) knock furthest; Sonic Wave skims them away low; Cream's Chao Cannon
and Eggman's cannon bounce them high; Tails' Energy Ball pops them up where they stood; the
Electric Bolt, the Beauty Shock and the Psycho Knife hold them up, floating down, a second or
so; Chaos' water splashes them a way off. Knuckles' specials raise no
dust and throw no bits: his rocks, Deep Impact, Major Eruption and Mole Bomb land on their
light, the jolt and the freeze alone. Sonic Meteor and Spiral Dive come down in dust; Girl
Tornado whirls wind round Amy; every trap goes off in Knuckles' Deep Impact explosion. Each
move's colour, flash, jolt, recoil, freeze and muzzle are in `SPECIALS` in `adventure.js`.

**Every blow lands too.** When a jab, heavy, upper, dash, air or aim attack connects, Sonic
Battle's own hit star pops where it met (its light one, or its heavy one for the finishers),
over a glow in the runner's colour, with a flash; the picture stops dead for a few hundredths of a second and jolts, the
jabs briefly and the finishers (the third jab, the heavies, the upper, the dash and aim
attacks) longest, while what was hit shows pure white. Then it is thrown, tumbling, the way
the blow sends it (an upper straight up), and bursts where it hits something or a moment
later. Knuckles hits hardest of the six, and Amy's hammer blows land like a hammer. A blow
that meets something in its wind-up lands there and then, so a heavy's step never carries
the runner into a hurt; the aim attack's dash does the same. A Spin Dash, roll or Homing
Attack that hits throws its mark the way it was going, a stomp sparks underfoot, and a
special's blast throws what it hits away from it. Each blow's freeze, jolt, spark and knock
are in `HITS` in `adventure.js`.

from above. Some cannot be hit every way: Senbon's spikes, a spinning Star and a shut Mirror
hurt whatever touches them, so wait till they open; Pierrot's ball and Gao-gao's face take
any blow, so jump on them or come in curled (or from behind); a blast (a special) beats them
all. Beaten, a badnik goes up in Sonic Advance's puff of smoke and its animal goes free.

The last seven stages have the badniks of TheKnucklesMainG4's
[Modern Sonic Badniks](https://www.deviantart.com/theknucklesmaing4/art/Badniks-Sprite-Sheet-%28Modern-Sonic-Generations%29-914913506) sheet (DeviantArt), the classic ones and the later
ones drawn as one set; `tools/pack-badniks.py` cuts the twenty-five the stages use from it
too. Each does what Sonic Retro says it does in its own game: Motobug drives to and fro;
Buzz Bomber and Buzzer fly over and fire down at forty-five degrees; Crabmeat stops to fire a
ball out of each claw; Chopper leaps up out of the water and Jawz swims to and fro;
Orbinaut's spike balls go round it, and come near and it fires them at you one by one
(touch one and it hurts); Nebula drops a bomb when you pass under it; Balkiry waits ahead,
then comes straight at you fast, and is gone; Snail Blaster crawls with its shell shut
(nothing hurts it) and opens it to fire twice; Butterdroid comes after you when you come
close, and so do the Spinners and the Klagen; Spiny lobs a spiked ball up at you; Slicer
throws its blades, which home in on you a while; Asteron flashes when you come near and goes
off in its five points; Grabber drops on you on its thread when you pass under; Grounder
waits behind a wall and bursts out through the bricks at you; Rhinobot charges what it sees;
Crawl holds its bumper out in front, so hit it from behind or above; the Electro Spinner and
Electro Klagen crackle with electricity now and then (don't touch them then), and the Metal
Spinner is armoured, broken only by a jump on it or a ball.

**The rules are the rings'.** Rings (in voxels) are your health: a hit throws them out of you
(thirty-two at most, to be caught again before they fade); a hit with none, a pit, or running
out of breath puts you back at the last Star Post. Item monitors -- Sonic Advance 3's, in its
sprites (`tools/pack-items.py`, ripped by SpitfyaUK and Techokami) -- hold a Super Ring (ten
rings), a Shield (it takes one hit), a Magnetic Shield (rings fly to you), Invincibility
(fifteen seconds, sparkles circling you like a sphere) or Speed Shoes. Springs are voxel,
Sonic Advance 3's red spring. Beaten badniks let their animal go. Each zone hides three Red
Star Rings across its Acts. The grade (S to C) weighs the time against the Act's par, the
share of rings, and whether you fell. Clearing a zone opens the next, and a zone once cleared
stays open; a clear from before the zones had Acts counts for both. Best times, grades, Red
Star Rings, the story's progress and Emerl's moves are kept in this browser
(`redringcircuit.save`); on a browser where the game was played as Post Parade's cabinet, that
record comes along the first time, and so do the settings and the two players' keys.

**The title screen** comes first. Behind it, far off and under a haze so it stays behind
everything else (its runner framed in the open band between Start and the strip, so nothing on
the title covers it), runs a demo: one of the cast at random plays a zone's first Act at random, by
itself, to the goal post (the post spins), then another runner in another zone, round and round.
A robot plays it honestly, keeping its speed as a player who knows the road would: it looks
further ahead the faster it goes for walls, hurdles, monitors, thorns, badniks, the end of the
road and water, and jumps each in good time for how tall it is, the jump planned before it's made
(the shortest that clears it and comes down with room to jump the next); runs up slopes and onto
what they lead to, plans tall walls from their steps, rolls through cracked rock (those who can
roll), goes over a badnik with its spikes out or an armoured face rather than into it (Chaos dives
under), never wades into water it can jump or fly over, holds a glide over a gap, Spin Dashes off
the line and out of a stop, and uses no attack that would stop it. It plays quietly; a runner it
can't get round a zone fairly isn't sent there, and a run that fails just ends and the next begins
(a run takes about half a minute). The same robot runs every CPU in a race. The stage it used is built
fresh before anyone plays it. Over it the Red Ring Circuit logo under its Sonic & Friends
banner and the login (below). Along the bottom, on a strip of checkered soil under grass, up to
four of the cast at a time come on at random, each to do one thing: walk across, run across,
come in and stand a while, rev a Spin Dash and roll off, fly over (Tails, Cream, Gamma; Eggman
on his Egg Mobile's side booster, its jets below being for going up), or glide over and drop
(Knuckles). Sonic Battle draws no walk, so Sonic's and
Knuckles' walks, and their waiting poses (Sonic's foot-tap and finger wag, Knuckles' look
about), are Sonic Advance's, in Sonic Battle's colours; Mighty's walk and pose and Metal
Sonic's pose are their own sheets' extras; the rest stand in their idle. Under it is the line
that says it is a fan-made game and the characters are SEGA's.

**Accounts.** The title is the login: a username and a password (six characters at least,
typed twice to make an account), remembered on this browser if you like. Accounts live in
this browser (the password salted and hashed); each has its own save (`redringcircuit.save.<id>`),
and the first one made takes the save that was here before. Your player card shows your
name and tag (`Name#TAG`, the friend code), level, and your record: battles, wins, KOs, races,
tournaments, and an online rating that moves with online battles. Your favourite fighter is your
picture everywhere (the top bar, the lobby, rooms, cards): pick it with the arrows beside it on
your own card, from your team; it's kept with your account and the others see it at once.

**Online** works the way the other games' lobbies do, with no server of its own: presence,
profiles, the lobby chat, the room list and private messages go over public MQTT brokers, each
player's key pair signing what they send and sealing their messages; a room links its players
straight to the host (PeerJS), with a relay over the brokers when that cannot connect. The first
time an account comes into the lobby it gets a **tour**: a spotlight on each part in turn (the
tabs, rooms, Quick match, hosting, joining by code, the players, the ping bars and how to turn
off the ones on name tags, the chat, friends and your card), a card beside it saying what it is,
Back, Next and Skip (Enter, Z or the right arrow on, the left arrow back, X out); the lobby's
Tour button plays it again. The
**lobby** lists the open rooms (Quick match joins one, or makes one), the players online (a
card each: challenge, invite, add as a friend, message, block) and the leaderboard, beside the
lobby chat. A **room** is for one of five things, set by its host, its players picking their
fighters, readying up and chatting (and quick lines). Beside each player's name are their ping
bars: the host times a round trip to every player every two seconds and shares the table, four
bars green under 60 ms, three under 120, two (orange) under 200, one (red) over that; the time
shows on hover, and the host's own are full (the room runs on their connection).
The lobby's player list has bars too, for each player's own connection: every game sends itself a
note through the brokers every ten seconds and times it there and back, and sends the result with
its presence (four bars under 150 ms, three under 250, two under 400, one over); yours shows by the
Online line at the top. Until a player's first measurement arrives their bars pulse grey. In a match, each
player's room bars can show on their tag too -- over their head and on their HUD card, and on the
other runners' name tags in races -- with Options' **Ping on player tags** (on at first; turn it off there).

- **Battle**: up to four, the host's arena, rules, lives or KOs, time and health, teams or not,
  CPUs to fill. The host runs the fight and sends it out twenty times a second; everyone's
  buttons go to the host. The results move the online rating.
- **Exhibition race**: up to eight through a zone's Act; everyone runs their own copy, the
  others shown as ghosts with their names, the host counting down and calling the order home.
- **Red Ring Circuit**: the grand prix for up to eight players, a cup and class the host picks,
  CPUs in the places nobody takes: the host's game runs them and the others see them where it
  says. Everyone runs their own copy of each course, the others as ghosts (a ghost, a player's or
  a CPU's, takes the rings and Red Rings it runs through on your course too, and lights the Star
  Posts); powers that reach another player go to them over the room (a hit on a CPU, to the host); the host gathers the times and sends the one result, so the points and
  the standings are everyone's. No retries online: the cup goes on whatever your place.
- **Story co-op**: up to three through a chapter of an episode: the host is its hero (a
  spectating host's: the first player), the others fight beside them, or run the stage alongside;
  a win clears the chapter on every player's save.
- **Tournament**: up to eight players in a bracket of 8 or 16, CPUs filling the rest; the
  host runs it, and every player's match is an online battle the room watches.

**Spectating.** A room has up to six spectator seats on top of its players' places. **Watch**
beside a room in the lobby takes one (a full room's only way in; a room whose places are all taken
seats anyone who asks to join as a spectator), and **Spectate** in a room moves you there (the host
too); **Play** moves you back if there's a place. A room whose match is already under way can be
watched too: Watch (or joining it at all) seats you and takes you straight into the match where
it has got to -- the battle as it stands, the race's clock and everyone's places, the grand prix's
race, CPUs and points (or its standings, between races), the tournament's bracket and its match,
the story's part (a scene: you watch from what comes after it). A spectator who goes back to the
room mid-match has **Watch the match** to go in again; a match that has just ended sends you back
to the room to watch the next. The host can move anyone either way (the eye and
person buttons on their row). Spectators pick no fighter and don't ready up. In the match a bar
along the bottom says who you're watching:

- a **battle** (a tournament's match or a story fight too, for everyone not in it): the whole
  arena, framed as ever, or one fighter followed close (the left and right arrows; Z back to the
  whole arena). Their HUD card is outlined.
- a **race**, the **grand prix** or a story run: any runner followed -- a player's ghost or a
  CPU, with their place (left and right) -- or a **free camera** (Z) that the arrows fly anywhere
  along the course (Z or X back to the runners, the nearest one). On the grand prix's strip the
  watched face is ringed.

A pad uses its d-pad and buttons the same way; a touch screen gets buttons on the bar. Nothing a
spectator watches counts: no XP, rating, wins, trophies or story chapters. With nobody playing,
the CPUs play for the spectators: a battle of two to four CPUs, a grand prix of eight, a bracket
of sixteen or eight (an exhibition race and story co-op need a player).

**Friends** (requests by friend code or from a card), **messages** (conversations kept on this
browser, read receipts) and invites to rooms work from anywhere in the game.

**Tournament** (the main menu, and online rooms) is a knock-out bracket of 8 or 16, Sonic
Battle's way: one battle a match, Survival with the lives set (one to three), three minutes,
200 health. You and the CPUs (levels around the one picked, fighters nobody took) are spread so
the players meet as late as they can; a winner moves on in the bracket as soon as their match
is over; a match between two CPUs is settled at once (their levels and some luck) or watched,
with a spectator's camera (follow either fighter, Left and Right, or Z for the whole arena). Going
out or winning it all gives its XP and goes on your record. Or **Spectate** it (You: Spectate, in
the setup): a bracket of CPUs only, each match watched or settled, nothing counted. The bracket
has its own music between matches; a match's stops when it ends.

**Sonic Battle's effects** are its own, from QuadFactor's rip of the game's shared effects ("Miscellaneous
Effects", The Spriters Resource; SEGA's sprites), cut by `tools/pack-effects.py`: the light
and heavy hit stars, the guard's barrier and the flash of a blow it turns aside, the healing
ring, the burst of a wall hit, the K.O. ring, the dust a heavy blow and a dash throw up, and the
pursuit attack's sweep.

**Controls.** Arrows or WASD to run (and Up and Down to pick a move, crouch or climb), Space
to jump (hold for height), Z, X and C to attack; in the grand prix, V or Shift uses your power. Enter goes on from the title to the main
menu, starts the Act from the zone select, and opens and closes the pause menu (Esc pauses
too). In the menus the arrows move between the choices -- to the nearest one that
way, with a gold outline round it -- Z confirms and X goes back: to the main menu from the zone select, back into the run from the pause menu, to the zone
select from the results. A gamepad works (stick or d-pad; A jumps,
X light, Y heavy, B special; the left shoulder the grand prix's power; Start pauses). On a phone there are buttons on screen -- a pad
of four, and Light, Heavy, Special and Jump (and Power in the grand prix) -- and the camera backs off on a tall screen so
twelve metres fit across.

**Stages are text** (`adventure-stages.js`): chunks of 32 rows, one character a one-metre
tile, with the legend at the top of the file (`E` is the left edge of a boss's arena). Each
zone is written as its two Acts, cut from the one stage at the Star Post between them.
`tools/genzones.py` (local) draws them, each zone from its own pieces, on the grand prix's
course pieces (`tools/gengp.py`: loops, corkscrews, cables, rails, pipes, cannons, currents,
wind, geysers, dust devils, low gravity) and `tools/genstages.py`'s older set pieces -- runs,
hills, climbs, pits with stepping stones, high roads, vaults, balloon ladders, bumper pits --
then the passes every zone gets (secret rooms, thorns and crevasses, badniks where there's room
to see them coming, more of each zone by zone), and writes the file, each Act with its
machinery; `node tools/validate.js` checks them,
and `tools/dev/adventure-bot.js` plays them through in a test page. Besides the old pieces
there are conveyor belts (`{` `}`), bumpers (`Q`) and balloons (`o`, which pop and throw you
up, your air moves back, as Sonic Advance's do). The stage is built in sixteen-tile voxel
chunks at the lobby's quarter-metre grain, with a deep top strip along the surface (grass, a
rug, snow, carpet, road with its lanes, marble squares, moss, sand, steel), each theme's own
blocks (chalk-grey stone, riveted steel with hazard stripes, books on end, carved stone with
glowing glyphs, dark glass edged in neon, concrete, white marble with a gilt band, mossy
carved stone, red sandstone in bands, burnt concrete with the fire in its cracks, hull
plating with its lights) and ledges (planks, grating, shelves, a gold rail, red girders), caves and roofed rooms backed with a wall, things that break or crumble as their
own meshes, rings as one instanced mesh, and each theme's scenery set back so the
perspective camera gives it parallax.
